LLM Hallucination

Why a model can produce a fluent continuation that is still factually wrong.

Two common failure patterns

A language model optimizes for a plausible continuation, not for truth by default. When similar patterns overlap in its learned representations, it can confidently combine details that do not belong together.

Case 1 · Misattribution
“The quarterly report says our revenue grew 18%.” Who approved the report?
The source does not name an approver, but the model may invent one because a name is statistically plausible.
Case 2 · Entity mixing
“Northstar uses the Atlas support knowledge base.” Which team owns Atlas?
The model may blend facts about two internal products if their descriptions frequently appear together.

The practical lesson

Fluency is not evidence. For factual work, ground the answer in trusted sources, require citations or structured evidence, and evaluate whether the model abstains when the source does not contain the answer.
